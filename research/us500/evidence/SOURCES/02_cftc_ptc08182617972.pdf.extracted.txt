 CONCISE EXPLANATION AND ANALYSIS OF THE PRODUCT AND ITS 
 COMPLIANCE WITH APPLICABLE PROVISIONS OF THE ACT, INCLUDING CORE 
 PRINCIPLES AND THE COMMISSION’S REGULATIONS THEREUNDER 
 Pursuant  to  Commission  Regulation  40.3(a)(4),  the  following  is  a  concise  explanation  and 
 analysis  of  the  product  and  its  compliance  with  the  Act,  including  the  relevant  Core  Principles 
 (discussed in Appendices E and F), and the Commission’s regulations thereunder. 
 I.  Introduction 
 The  US500  Contract  is  a  perpetual  futures  contract  on  the  MerQube  US  Large  Cap  Index 
 (Bloomberg:  MQ5C;  Reuters:  .MQ5C),  a  broad-based  securities  index  composed  of  the 
 500  largest  companies  listed  and  domiciled  in  the  United  States,  weighted  by 
 float-adjusted market capitalization and administered by MerQube. 
 What  is  a  perpetual  future?  A  perpetual  future  is  a  cash-settled  futures  contract  with  no 
 fixed  expiration  or  delivery  date.  Rather  than  converging  to  a  deliverable  cash  price  on  a 
 stated  settlement  day,  it  remains  economically  tethered  to  a  continuously  observed 
 reference  through  a  periodic  funding  mechanism:  at  regular  intervals,  holders  of  the  side 
 trading  at  a  premium  to  the  reference  pay  holders  of  the  side  trading  at  a  discount,  so  that 
 any  divergence  between  the  contract’s  mark  and  the  underlying  reference  generates  an 
 offsetting  carrying  cost  that  draws  the  two  back  into  alignment.  The  instrument  therefore 
 functions  as  an  indefinitely  renewing  near-dated  future,  allowing  a  participant  to  hold  a 
 position  open  for  as  long  as  the  underlying  exposure  persists  without  rolling  from  one 
 expiration to the next. 
 Why  are  these  important?  Perpetual  futures  have  become  the  dominant  form  of 
 derivative  and  a  principal  locus  of  price  discovery  in  the  markets  in  which  they  have 
 emerged,  yet  that  activity  has  historically  occurred  almost  entirely  on  offshore, 
 unregulated  venues.  Listing  a  perpetual  on  a  CFTC-registered  DCM  brings  this 
 economically  significant  activity  into  a  regulated  environment  featuring  trade 
 surveillance,  know-your-customer  verification,  risk-based  margin,  central  clearing,  and 
 disciplinary  procedures  consistent  with  the  CEA,  protecting  U.S.  participants  and 
 preserving  U.S.  price  discovery  rather  than  ceding  both  abroad.  For  investors  whose 
 exposure  to  the  broad  U.S.  equity  market  is  itself  continuous  and  indefinite—asset 
 managers  and  institutional  investors  maintaining  long-horizon  exposure  to  U.S.  large-cap 
 equities,  funds  tracking  or  benchmarked  to  a  broad  large-cap  index,  participants 
 managing  ongoing  equity-beta  exposure,  and  dealers  warehousing  equity-index  risk  from 
 client  facilitation—a  perpetual  is  structurally  superior  to  a  strip  of  dated  futures:  it 
 eliminates  roll  cost  and  roll-date  basis  risk,  tracks  the  reference  more  tightly  through  the 
 funding  mechanism,  and  concentrates  liquidity  in  a  single  instrument  rather  than 
 fragmenting  it  across  expirations.  Listing  a  regulated  domestic  perpetual  thus  directly

 advances  the  Commission’s  stated  objective  of  onshoring  novel  derivatives  products  that

 Perpetuals  are  an  especially  natural  fit  for  a  broad  equity  market  index.  Of  the 
 things  on  which  a  derivative  can  be  written,  a  broad  equity  market  index  is  among  the 
 most  natural  candidates  for  a  perpetual,  indefinitely  renewing  structure,  because  the 
 exposure  it  represents—participation  in  a  diversified  basket  of  the  market’s  largest 
 companies—is  itself  continuous  and  has  no  terminal  date.  There  is  always  a  prevailing 
 level  of  the  U.S.  large-cap  market;  the  economic  variable  does  not  mature,  expire,  or 
 settle  on  a  delivery  day.  The  Underlying  is  the  level  of  the  index  itself—not  a  dated 
 delivery  price—so,  unlike  a  dated  equity  index  future,  it  does  not  carry  a  fixed  expiration 
 to  which  it  must  converge,  roll  down  a  basis  to  that  expiration,  or  require  participants  to 
 migrate  from  one  listed  month  to  the  next.  A  perpetual  on  that  index  therefore  mirrors  the 
 economic  reality  of  continuous,  long-horizon  equity  exposure  far  more  closely  than  the 
 existing  exchange-traded  tools  for  taking  or  hedging  it.  Dated  equity  index  futures,  such 
 as  the  quarterly  E-mini  contracts  on  major  U.S.  large-cap  benchmarks,  must  be  rolled 
 every  calendar  quarter,  incurring  roll  cost,  roll-date  basis  risk,  and  the  operational  burden 
 of  managing  the  front-month-to-back-month  transition.  The  Contract  collapses  that 
 maintenance  into  a  single,  continuously  held  instrument  that  tracks  the  current  index  level 
 without  any  roll.  Where  a  participant’s  information  instead  lives  in  the  term 
 structure—calendar  spreads,  the  path  of  expected  dividends  and  financing  to  specific 
 tenors,  or  date-certain  settlement  needs—dated  equity  index  instruments  remain  valuable, 
 so that the perpetual and dated structures are complementary rather than substitutes. 
 The  funding  mechanism  has  a  well-defined  economic  interpretation  for  an  equity 
 index.  Because  the  Contract  synthesizes  a  continuously  held,  financed  position  in  the 
 underlying  large-cap  basket,  its  equilibrium  funding  is  not  zero  but  reflects  the  net  cost  of 
 carrying  that  position—the  short-term  financing  rate  on  the  capital  required  to  hold  the 
 basket,  less  the  dividend  yield  accruing  to  the  constituent  securities.  Because  the 
 Underlying  is  a  price-return  index,  which  excludes  reinvested  dividends,  a  holder  of  the 
 Contract  does  not  itself  receive  the  dividends  paid  by  the  constituents;  the  funding 
 mechanism  is  accordingly  the  perpetual  analog  of  the  cost-of-carry  basis  embedded  in  a 
 dated  equity  index  future,  passing  this  financing-minus-dividend  differential  between  the 
 two  sides  at  each  Periodic  Transfer  so  as  to  keep  the  Contract’s  traded  level  tethered  to 
 the  index.  For  prevailing  financing  rates  and  dividend  yields  this  per-period  carry  is 
 small—on  the  order  of  the  net  carry  accruing  over  a  single  day,  a  small  fraction  of  one 
 percent  of  the  index  level.  The  Contract’s  deadband  is  accordingly  set  below  the  typical 
 per-period  carry,  so  that  it  filters  microstructure  noise  without  suppressing  the  genuine 
 carry,  while  the  clamp  is  set  far  above  it  and  functions  as  a  manipulation  and  tail-risk 
 backstop rather than a routine constraint. 
 The  Contract  trades  on  the  twenty-four-hour,  five-day  schedule  of  the  U.S. 
 equity-index  futures  market,  while  its  funding  mechanics  are  anchored  to  the 
 real-time  level  of  the  Index  during  regular  U.S.  equity  trading  hours.  The  Underlying 
 Price  Index  is  the  level  of  the  Index  as  calculated  and  disseminated  by  the  Source  Agency

 Each  Contract  represents  a  continuous  obligation  to  make  or  receive  cash  payments  based 
 on  changes  in  the  Contract’s  mark  and  periodic  funding  payments.  Positions  are  marked 
 to  market  on  a  continuous  basis,  and  margin  is  collected  and  paid  in  accordance  with  the 
 Exchange’s  margin  methodology.  During  the  time  that  trading  on  the  Contract  is  open, 
 Members  are  able  to  adjust  their  positions  and  trade  freely.  Members  may  enter  and  exit 
 positions  at  any  time  during  trading  hours  by  executing  offsetting  trades.  A  Member 
 holding  a  long  position  profits  when  the  Index  level  rises  and  incurs  losses  when  it  falls;  a 
 Member  holding  a  short  position  is  affected  in  the  opposite  manner.  To  maintain 
 convergence  with  the  Underlying,  the  Contract  employs  a  periodic  funding  mechanism 
 whereby  long  and  short  holders  exchange  funding  payments  based  on  the  difference 
 between  the  Contract’s  mark  and  the  Underlying  Price  Index,  as  specified  in  Appendix  A. 
 A  Member’s  gain  or  loss  at  any  point  is  equal  to  the  change  in  the  Contract’s  mark  since 
 the  position  was  established,  multiplied  by  the  Contract  Multiplier,  plus  or  minus  net 
 funding  payments  received  or  paid.  Settlement  of  all  payments  is  made  in  cash;  no 
 physical delivery occurs.

 Scope:  These rules shall apply to this Contract. 
 Underlying:  The Underlying for this Contract is the  MerQube US Large Cap Index (Bloomberg: 
 MQ5C; Reuters: .MQ5C), a broad-based, float-market-capitalization-weighted, price-return 
 securities index composed of the 500 largest companies listed and domiciled in the United 
 States, as calculated and administered by MerQube. The Contract is quoted in Index points. A 
 long position in the Contract increases in value when the level of the Index rises and decreases in 
 value when the level of the Index falls; a short position is affected in the opposite manner. 
 Underlying Price Index:  For a given time t, the Underlying  Price Index (UPIt) is the most 
 recent level of the Index as calculated and disseminated in real time by the Source Agency. 
 During any period in which the Source Agency reports the Index as not being calculated 
 (including outside regular U.S. equity trading hours, on days that are not Index Business Days, 
 and during any suspension of calculation), or reports the feed as stale or unavailable, the 
 Underlying Price Index shall be the last level published while calculation was live. Whether the 
 Index is being calculated, and whether the feed is current, is determined as reported by the 
 Source Agency. 
 Type:  The type of Contract is a Perpetual Futures  Contract. 
 Source Agency:  The Source Agency is MerQube. MerQube  is the Index Sponsor, Index 
 Administrator, and Index Calculation Agent for the Index, and MerQube UK Limited is the 
 Benchmark Administrator for the MerQube US Index Series under the United Kingdom 
 Benchmarks Regulation. MerQube calculates and disseminates the level of the Index in real time 
 during regular U.S. equity trading hours on each Index Business Day, from the prices of the 
 constituent securities on their primary listing exchanges, and separately calculates and publishes 
 an official closing level of the Index for each Index Business Day from the official closing prices 
 of the constituent securities, in each case subject to MerQube's automated validation and 
 quality-control procedures. The real-time series and the official closing level are computed under 
 the same methodology, constituent set, and divisor. Appendix D describes the Source Agency in 
 detail. A new Source Agency may be added or substituted via a Part 40 amendment. 
 Trading Hours:  The Contract trades continuously from  6:00 P.M. Eastern Time on Sunday to 
 5:00 P.M. Eastern Time on Friday, throughout the lifetime of the Contract, subject to any trading 
 halt or maintenance window imposed by the Exchange. This schedule corresponds to the weekly 
 trading session of the U.S. equity-index futures market: like the E-mini equity-index futures 
 listed on CME, the Contract trades continuously through each weekday and overnight and is 
 closed over the weekend, when the U.S. equity market and its futures market are closed; the 
 Contract trades continuously through each weekday session without a daily maintenance halt. 
 Funding and settlement, however, are keyed to the Index, which is calculated only during regular 
 trading hours on Index Business Days; accordingly, Premiums are computed only during such 
 hours, and Periodic Transfers, Funding Rate determinations, and Settlement Price determinations 
 occur only on Index Business Days, as provided below. An “Index Business Day” is a day on 
 which the primary U.S. securities exchanges on which the Index’s constituents are listed—the

 is scheduled to be calculated. 
 Contract Multiplier:  US$1.00 per Index point. Each  Contract has a notional value equal to the 
 level of the Index multiplied by US$1.00. 
 Price Quotation:  Prices for the Contract are quoted  in Index points (e.g., 8360.00). 
 Minimum Tick:  The Minimum Tick size for this Contract  shall be 0.05 Index points. 
 Tick Value:  A one-tick price movement equals  $0.05  per Contract  , calculated as the Minimum 
 Tick multiplied by the Contract Multiplier (0.05 × US$1.00 = $0.05). 
 Minimum Unit:  The minimum unit of trading is one ten-thousandth  of one Contract, unless 
 otherwise specified in a Contract’s terms and conditions or on the Contract’s market page. 
 Position Accountability Level:  $25,000,000 mark-to-market  position value (in the case of a 
 short position, the equivalent long position value is considered). 
 Position Limits:  The Contract is subject to the Position  Accountability Level set forth herein 
 and to the position accountability framework of the Exchange Rulebook. Because the Underlying 
 is a broad-based security index — an excluded commodity under Section 1a(19) of the CEA — 
 the Contract is not subject to federal speculative position limits under 17 CFR Part 150, which 
 applies to physical-commodity contracts linked to the core referenced futures contracts 
 enumerated in Regulation 150.2(d); the Contract is cash-settled, has no delivery obligation, and 
 has no expiration and therefore no spot month. The Exchange may, consistent with Commission 
 standards and applicable filing requirements, supplement this accountability framework with 
 exchange-set position limits as open interest develops. 
 Periodic Transfer Criterion:  At each Periodic Transfer  Time, the Funding Rate is computed as 
 defined below. If the Funding Rate is zero, no Periodic Transfer is made. If the Funding Rate is 
 positive, long-side holders must contribute an amount equal to the Funding Rate multiplied by 
 the Settlement Price at the Periodic Transfer Time, multiplied by the Contract Multiplier 
 (US$1.00 per Index point), to short-side holders, for each Contract held. If the Funding Rate is 
 negative, short-side holders must contribute an amount equal to the absolute value of the 
 Funding Rate multiplied by the Settlement Price at the Periodic Transfer Time, multiplied by the 
 Contract Multiplier (US$1.00 per Index point), to long-side holders, for each Contract held. 
 Revisions to the Underlying made after any Periodic Transfer Time will not be accounted for in 
 determining the applicable Periodic Transfer. 
 Periodic Transfer Time:  The Periodic Transfer Time  is 4:00 P.M. Eastern Time on each Index 
 Business Day or, on any Index Business Day on which the primary U.S. securities exchanges 
 close early, the scheduled early close of regular trading. No Periodic Transfer, Funding Rate 
 determination, or Settlement Price determination occurs on a day that is not an Index Business 
 Day; in that case the most recent Settlement Price and published Index level remain in effect 
 until the next Index Business Day. Each Funding Period runs from the immediately preceding 
 Periodic Transfer Time to the current Periodic Transfer Time.

 Premium Index:  For each minute during which the Source Agency reports the Index as being 
 calculated and the feed as current, the Exchange computes a per-minute Premium as follows: 
 Premium(t) = ( Perpetual Price(t) − Reference Price(t) ) / Reference Price(t) 
 where Perpetual Price(t) is the volume-weighted average price of the Contract during the 
 applicable one-minute interval; if no trades in the Contract occurred during that interval, the 
 Premium for that interval is undefined and shall be excluded from the Funding Rate calculation; 
 and Reference Price(t) is the time-weighted average of the Underlying Price Index over the 
 applicable one-minute interval. 
 Funding Rate:  Let Premium(1), Premium(2), …, Premium(N)  be the per-minute Premiums 
 computed during the Funding Period, where N is the number of one-minute intervals during the 
 Funding Period for which a Premium was computed (excluding any interval excluded under 
 Reference Market Closed Periods, Reference Market Halts and Feed Outages, or any other 
 provision herein, and any interval during which no trades in the Contract occurred). The Funding 
 Rate shall equal the Weighted Average Premium Index (WAPI) of those per-minute Premiums, 
 computed by weighting each Premium equally: Funding Rate = ( Σ from k=1 to N of Premium(k) 
 ) / N. For the avoidance of doubt, this is an equally weighted (simple) average of the computed 
 per-minute Premiums: each Premium computed during the Funding Period receives identical 
 weight regardless of its chronological position, and no Premium is weighted by recency. If N is 
 zero, the Funding Rate shall be zero. The Funding Rate is subject to the following adjustments: 
 (a) Deadband: If the absolute value of the Funding Rate falls below 0.002%, the Funding 
 Rate shall be set to zero. 
 (b) Clamp: If the absolute value of the Funding Rate exceeds 2.00%, the Funding Rate 
 shall be clamped to +2.00% or −2.00%, as applicable (the "Maximum Funding 
 Magnitude").

 Tier 1 (Trade VWAP).  If one or more trades in the  Contract occur during the sixty

 For purposes of Tiers 2 and 3, sixty (60) Observation Points are defined at exactly N 
 seconds before the Periodic Transfer Time for each integer N from 1 to 60. An 
 Observation Point is “two-sided” if both a prevailing best bid and a prevailing best 
 ask rest on the order book at that instant, and its Midpoint is the arithmetic mean of 
 the two. 
 Tier 2 (Sampled Midpoint Average).  If no trades in  the Contract occur during the 
 Settlement Period and at least one Observation Point is two-sided, the Settlement 
 Price shall be the arithmetic mean of the Midpoints at all two-sided Observation 
 Points. 
 Tier 3 (Index Net Change).  If no trades in the Contract  occur during the Settlement 
 Period and no Observation Point is two-sided, the Settlement Price shall equal the 
 prior Settlement Price plus the net change in the Underlying Price Index between the 
 prior Periodic Transfer Time and the current Periodic Transfer Time.

 participants  with  bona  fide  economic  exposure  to  the  U.S.  large-cap  equity  market.  The  U.S. 
 equity  market  is  the  largest  and  most  liquid  equity  market  in  the  world,  and  the  500  companies 
 that  constitute  the  Index  represent  the  large-capitalization  segment  of  that  market,  with  an 
 aggregate  market  capitalization  in  the  tens  of  trillions  of  U.S.  dollars  and  daily  share-trading

 A  wide  range  of  participants  carry  continuous,  indefinite-duration  exposure  to  the  U.S.  large-cap 
 equity  market  that  a  perpetual  futures  contract  is  uniquely  suited  to  hedge  or  express.  Asset

 continuously  held  instrument  that  tracks  the  prevailing  level  of  the  Index  without  a  quarterly  roll

 expirations.  Because  the  Underlying  is  the  level  of  the  Index  itself,  the  Contract  does  not  carry  a 
 fixed  expiration  to  which  it  must  converge,  so  a  holder  is  not  exposed  to  the  roll  and

 Resilience and Manipulation Resistance 
 The  rules-based,  float-capitalization-weighted  construction  of  the  Index  provides  inherent 
 resistance  to  manipulation  through  any  single  constituent.  The  Index  aggregates  the  exchange 
 prices  of  500  independently  traded  large-capitalization  securities,  each  of  which  trades  in  the 
 deepest  and  most  liquid  equity  market  in  the  world  and  is  separately  subject  to  the  surveillance 
 and  listing  standards  of  its  primary  exchange  and  to  the  federal  securities  laws.  Because  the 
 Index  is  float-capitalization-weighted  across  500  constituents,  no  single  constituent  represents 
 more  than  a  small  fraction  of  the  Index,  so  that  distorting  the  Index  level  would  require  moving 
 the  prices  of  a  large  number  of  highly  liquid  securities  simultaneously—a  prohibitively  costly

 Core  Principles  7  and  8  -  Availability  of  General  Information  and  Daily  Publication  of 
 Trading  Information:  Core  Principles  7  and  8,  implemented  by  Regulations  Sections  38.400, 
 38.401,  38.450,  and  38.451,  require  a  DCM  to  make  available  to  the  public  accurate  information 
 regarding  the  contract  terms  and  conditions,  daily  information  on  contracts  such  as  settlement 
 price,  volume,  open  interest,  and  opening  and  closing  ranges,  the  rules,  regulations,  and 
 mechanisms  for  executing  transactions  on  or  through  the  facilities  of  the  contract  market,  and  the 
 rules  and  specifications  describing  the  operation  of  the  contract  market’s  electronic  matching 
 platform. 
 Rule  2.17  of  the  Rulebook  sets  forth  the  rules  for  publicizing  information.  The  Rulebook  and  the 
 specifications  of  each  contract  listed  on  the  Exchange,  including  the  Contract,  are  made  public 
 on  the  Exchange  website  and  remain  accessible  via  the  platform.  The  Exchange  will  post 
 non-confidential  materials  associated  with  regulatory  filings,  including  the  Rulebook,  at  the  time 
 the  Exchange  submits  such  filings  to  the  Commission.  Consistent  with  Rule  2.17  of  the 
 Rulebook,  the  Exchange  website  will  publish  contract  specifications,  terms,  and  conditions,  as 
 well  as  daily  trading  volume  and  open  interest  for  the  Contract.  Each  contract  has  a  dedicated 
 “Market  Page”  on  the  Kalshi  Exchange  platform,  which  will  contain  the  information  described 
 above  as  well  as  a  link  to  the  Underlying.  Chapter  5  sets  forth  the  rules,  regulations  and 
 mechanisms  for  executing  transactions,  and  the  rules  and  specifications  for  Kalshi’s  trading 
 systems. 
 Core  Principle  11  -  Financial  Integrity  of  Transactions:  Each  Member  must  be  in  good 
 standing  and  in  compliance  with  the  Member  eligibility  standards  set  forth  in  Chapter  3  of  the 
 Rulebook.  All  contracts  offered  by  the  Exchange,  including  the  Contract,  are  cleared  through  the 
 Clearinghouse,  a  Derivatives  Clearing  Organization  (“DCO”)  registered  with  the  CFTC  and 
 subject  to  all  CFTC  Regulations  related  thereto.  The  Exchange  requires  that  Members  post  initial 
 margin  before  entering  into  positions  and  maintain  maintenance  margin  throughout  the  life  of  a 
 position.  Margin  requirements  are  calculated  using  the  Exchange’s  risk-based  margin 
 methodology  and  are  designed  to  ensure  that  the  Exchange  can  liquidate  positions  in  an  orderly 
 manner  before  losses  exceed  posted  collateral.  Positions  that  fall  below  maintenance  margin 
 levels  are  subject  to  liquidation.  The  protection  of  customer  funds  is  monitored  by  the  Exchange 
 and  ensured  by  the  Clearinghouse  as  member  property  or  futures  commission  merchant  customer 
 property. 
 All  Remaining  Requirements:  All  remaining  Core  Principles  are  satisfied  through  operation  of 
 the Exchange’s Rules, processes, and policies applicable to the other contracts traded thereon.
